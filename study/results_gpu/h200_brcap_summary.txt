== Interpretation (not measured directly; no hardware counters were used) ==
I1 The 1.1-1.16 IPC "cap" of br_every1 is mostly a code-size effect: the same branch instructions reach 1.58-1.60 BRA/clk in a
   1.6-3.2 KB loop. Dependence only from 2 warps per block on, and the cliff between 6.2 and 9.3 KB at 32 warps (3.2-6.2 KB at 16),
   fit a per-processing-block instruction cache of limited capacity that thrashes when several warps sit at different points of a
   large loop. This is an inference from timing only.
I2 The resource is per processing block (F2 knees at multiples of 4, F5 no cross-block interference, 2 blocks of branch warps get
   exactly half of 4 blocks). An SM-wide shared front end is not what limits these rates.
I3 Branches do not take integer or FP issue capacity beyond their own slot (F4). Within a block, an FMA co-runner that can issue
   every cycle shuts the branch warps out (F6); an integer co-runner and branch warps slow each other more than slot counts
   explain. Neither pattern is explained by the four candidates.
I4 Simulator comparison: the hardware rate to compare a simulator WITHOUT an instruction-cache capacity effect against is the
   small-loop rate. Branch-only loop, SM IPC / BRA per clk:
        warps        8              16             32             64 (= older 32 warps, F8)

     3.2 KB x32   0.640/0.571    1.177/1.050    1.797/1.603    1.793/1.600
     12.4 KB 128  0.568/0.551    0.900/0.873    1.115/1.082    1.111/1.077
   Simulator br_every1 (128 branches) final settings 0.40/0.80/1.59/1.94 IPC (= 0.39/0.78/1.54/1.88 BRA/clk), before 0.35/0.70/1.28/1.30.
   Against the large-loop rate the final simulator is high at 32 and 64 warps (+43%, +75% in BRA/clk) and low at 8 and 16;
   against the small-loop rate it is low at 8 (-30%) and 16 (-22 to -26%), about equal at 32 (-3%), and high at 64 (+18%),
   where the part runs only the older CTA's 32 warps (whether the simulator's 64-warp layout has 2 CTAs is not known here).
   A "branch-dense overshoot" holds only against the large-loop (cache-limited) rate.
