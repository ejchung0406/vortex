   They reach the same ceiling as the checked rows, but in layout w1 they run a shorter loop (no check), hence 77 instead of 66 B/clk.
D3 Layouts wn / cn do not exist for 32 KB N >= 8 and 16 KB N = 16 (no room); the full 1..16 range at 32 KB exists only aliased (w1, w2, w4).
D4 "outstanding" is issue to DETECTED completion: an upper bound, inflated by the wait form (nanosleep(100) adds several hundred cycles).
D5 A8 (rate falling with many unbounded small transfers) has large spread and aliased destinations (1-8 slots per warp); cause not found.
D6 The lat1 times have a 46-cycle poll granularity; DRAM times vary (p90 = 1.3-1.6 x median).
D7 Carve-out 50 behaves as 100; which shared-memory size the driver chose was not read back. L1 capacity under each setting is only
   bracketed (>= 128 KB and < 256 KB at default / 0; >= 16 KB and < 32 KB at 50 / 100).
D8 One die, one SM (SM 0; SM 1 as the second sample in multi-CTA rows). L2 residency of the 8 MB source is assumed from its size.

== Interpretation (not measured directly) ==
I1 No plateau that would indicate a small number of transfer engines: 16 transfers progress concurrently at an unchanged per-transfer
   rate (A2), and a single large transfer alone already streams at 1.67 lines/clk, the same as the many-transfer ceiling (A5 vs A1).
   What limits TMA on one SM is one shared delivery path of about 107 B/clk plus a fixed cost per transfer (340 cycles L2, ~500-850
   DRAM) and the issuing thread's own loop (104-119 cycles without a wait, 290-300 with try_wait).
I2 The TMA-alone ceiling here (105-108 B/clk, 208-214 GB/s) is below the 110-116 B/clk the notes give for TMA + loads together and
   above the notes' TMA-alone 196-204 GB/s (one issuer 32 KB x 6 / two issuers x 3, which A4 shows were partly issue-bound).
I3 L1 hits are limited to 128 B per clock = two 64 B lines (or four 32 B sectors) per clock, independent of the load width; it is
   not an instruction-issue limit (B2). In lane-loads: 32 / 16 / 8 per clock for 4 / 8 / 16 B.

(a) sums to 1.0: stores and loads of one SM share ONE budget of 1.000 warp-instruction per clock at 4 B (128 B per clock at any element
width), not 1.0 each. The split between the two kinds is not 50:50 and depends on which warps do what (0.40:0.60 interleaved,
0.56:0.44 in halves; 1:3 gives 0.20:0.80, 3:1 gives 0.667:0.333).

-- Interpretation (not measured directly) --
J1 M3 fits a per-processing-block limit under the SM-wide one, if warp index mod 4 selects the block (the brcap notes found that for
   %warpid; not re-checked here): stores 0.25 per clock per block (= one warp's 4 cycles per store), loads 0.5 per clock per block,
   and 1.0 per clock for the SM over both kinds.
J2 TMA writes into shared memory are outside the loads' budget (loads lose 0.006-0.023 per clock while TMA writes 21-76 B/clk) but
   they contend with stores (stores fall to 0.64, TMA to less than half).
